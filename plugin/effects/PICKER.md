# Effects picker: which effect, when, and why

`CATALOG.md` says what each effect is. This file says which one to reach for, given what the line is doing.
Every row of a beat sheet names its effect AND a one-line reason. The reasons below are editing judgment, not
measured results.

## How to pick, in this order

1. **What is the line doing?** Find its job below: hook, list, "you don't need", process, proof, change, story,
   punch word, call to action.
2. **Take the first pick** unless its "skip when" applies, then take the backup.
3. **Check the rhythm:** a new visual about every 4 seconds, never the same kind twice in a row, at most one
   footage effect (grade, film look, wipe) every 10 seconds or so, and at most one punch-in every 5 seconds.
4. **Match the reel:** teaching reels can run dense. Ads and sales videos run light: real footage first, and an
   effect only where it fixes something you can point at.
5. **Write what you do on camera** for each effect (say the cue, hold still, look at it, leave headroom), so the
   shoot gives the edit what it needs.

## By what the line is doing

### The hook (0 to 2 s): stop the scroll and say who it's for
| Pick | Why | Skip when | On camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`kinetic-hook-behind`**: a word behind your head | The depth (word behind you, you in front) looks expensive in a feed of flat captions, and the word reads before the audio does. | No headroom, or you're close to the wall (the cut-out gets messy). | Top of your head a third of the way down, 2 m off the wall. |
| `cover-title` | Reads with the sound off; the accent box carries the promise. | A face-led emotional hook. Let your face do it. | Nothing. |
| `title-card-shrink` | Feels like an episode opening. Good for a series or a longer teaching reel. | Reels under 30 s (it spends 2 s not talking). | Start talking on the cut, not before. |

### A list or steps ("3 things", "5 tools", "4 steps")
| Pick | Why | Skip when | On camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`chapter-tracker`** | Shows how many are left, so people stay for the last one, and every advance is a small visual change for free. | Fewer than 3 items, or a story with no list. | Say the count first ("five things"). Keep that side of the frame clear. |
| `step-card`, one per item | Each item gets its own moment and a one-word title people can screenshot. | Items too long for one word. | Number each item out loud. |
| `redact-list` | The blank bars make people wait for each word. | Fewer than 3 items. | Say the count first. |
| `step-stack` | A fast recap of 2 to 6 one-word steps. | When each step needs explaining. | Say the words at an even pace. |
| `storyboard-deck` | For "here's the whole plan": shows the volume at a glance. | When each item needs explaining. | Gesture across the bottom of frame. |
| `split-card` + the speaker-split recipe | Keeps your face on screen while the points build beside you. | Tight selfie framing. | 2 or 3 short points, said straight after each other. |

### "You don't need X", "stop doing X", "delete these"
| Pick | Why | Skip when | On camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`jiggle-delete`** | Literal and satisfying: the apps wobble and vanish, so "delete these" happens on screen. | Fewer than 3 things, or they aren't apps. | Pause a beat after "delete these". |
| `icon-strike` | Faster, about 1.5 s, for a quick "you don't need X or Y" in passing. | When the strike would land on a tool the viewer probably loves. | Name the things out loud. |

### Showing how it works ("I just...")
| Pick | Why | Skip when | On camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`prompt-bar`** | Makes "I told Claude to..." concrete: they see the exact input. Pair it with a real result next. | The prompt is long. Use `prompt-flash` and give the full prompt away elsewhere. | Say the prompt in plain words. |
| `prompt-card` | For a prompt you want people to read and copy. | Under 3 s of screen time to spare. | Hold the line while it reveals. |
| `file-drop` | "I just drop the clips in": shows the effort is one step. | When the input isn't a file. | Mime the drop if you like. |
| `editing-timeline` | "No editing software": shows the thing they won't have to touch. | Non-video topics. | Nothing. |

### Proof: show it, don't claim it
| Pick | Why | Skip when | On camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A real screen recording, cropped tight, with `path-ring`** | The strongest proof is the real thing; the ring tells the eye where to look. | Names, numbers or spend you haven't cleared to show. Blur them or skip. | "Here's what it looks like." |
| `stat-card` | A number lands harder big and alone, and the source chip answers "says who". | Unsourced or made-up numbers. | Say the number slowly. |
| `quote-card` | Someone else saying it beats you saying it. | You can't name who said it and where. | Nothing. |
| `proof-card` | A real headline backs a claim about the world. | No real headline to show. | Nothing. |
| `clock-counter` | Makes "start to finish in X" visible. | Times you didn't actually record. | Say the start and the finish. |
| `claim-bars` | Shows a gap between what's claimed and what's proven, without inventing a number. | When you do have the real numbers (use `stat-card`). | Lean on "back up". |
| `filmstrip` | "It's only as good as your footage": shows the raw takes behind the edit. | Fake or stock frames. | Nothing. |

### A change or a difference (before and after)
| Pick | Why | Skip when | On camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`grade-wipe`** | Same frame, only the change moves, so the eye can't miss it. Best for anything visual: a grade, a look, raw vs edited. | Non-visual changes (use `ab-split`). | Hold still for 2 s. |
| `ab-split` | For states or numbers: the old way cracks and falls, the new way slides in. | No real "before". | Say "before" and "after". |
| `eyedropper-grade` | Cause and effect in one move: pick colours, the picture changes. Fits "match my brand". | Any other topic. | Point at something in frame. |

### Speed and competition
| Pick | Why | Skip when | On camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`two-phones`** | "Whoever answers first wins" in one picture. | Nothing to do with response time. | Look at where the phones will be. |
| `phone-race` | "Everyone's chasing the same customer." | When you'd need real competitor names. | Nothing. |

### Story, mood, a personal moment
| Pick | Why | Skip when | On camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The film-look recipe** on the story part, back to normal for the lesson | Signals "this is a story" without saying it; switching back says "now the point". | Teaching beats (it softens the message). | Change your energy with it. |
| A full cutaway for 1 to 2 s while you keep talking | Resets attention on a punch line. | More than once every 8 s or so. | Nothing. |

### The punch word
| Pick | Why | Skip when | On camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`punch-word`**, full frame, hard cut | Makes one word the thing they remember. At most one every 5 s. | Words that aren't the point. | Lean on the word. |
| `gravity-drop` | Turns loss words into motion: gone, dead, dropped, lost. The letters falling ARE the word. | Positive words. | A beat after the word. |
| `highlighter` | A claim worth a second read. | Every line. | Nothing. |
| `price-bounce` | "AI can't price this job": the machine gets bounced, you type it. | Anything that would show a real price. | Nothing. |

### The call to action
| Pick | Why | Skip when | On camera |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`comment-drop` mid-reel, then `comment-reply` and `comment-cta` at the end** | Shows exactly what to type AND what happens after, which removes the "what if I comment and nothing happens" doubt. | You don't have a working, tested auto-reply for that word. Then use "follow for the next one". | Say the word slowly, midway and at the end. |
| `share-card` | "Send this to someone who..." turns viewers into distribution. | When nobody obvious would get it sent to them. | Say who to send it to. |

## Footage that makes these possible

- **Waist up, top of your head a third of the way down, 2 m off the wall:** unlocks the word behind your head,
  side cards, the chapter tracker.
- **4K:** unlocks punch-ins without blur.
- **2 seconds of the empty room at the end:** a clean background for cut-out effects.
- **Hold still for 1 to 2 seconds** wherever a footage effect lands (wipe, eyedropper).
- **Say the effect out loud while you film** ("zoom in here", "put the three steps up here"). Claude finds those
  words in the transcript and puts the effect right on them.
